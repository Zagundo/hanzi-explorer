# Hanzi Explorer Strategy

## The problem

Learning Chinese characters can become fragmented across flashcards, dictionaries, course apps, handwritten notes, and isolated vocabulary lists. These tools help with recall or reference, but they rarely help a learner build a personal, visual understanding of how characters carry form, meaning, history, and relationships.

Hanzi Explorer exists to make each learned character feel like a lasting object of knowledge rather than another item in a review queue.

## Our approach

Hanzi Explorer is a personal character atlas: a growing collection of visual specimens that connect a character’s form, pronunciation, meaning, components, historical origins, modern usage, and personal associations.

The experience should feel closer to exploring a museum cabinet than using a spreadsheet, dictionary, or flashcard deck.

The product grows alongside Simon’s Mandarin learning. Characters enter the atlas because they have become meaningful through study, curiosity, or use—not because the app is trying to become a comprehensive dictionary.

## Who it is for

The primary user is Simon: a visually motivated Mandarin learner who wants to understand characters deeply and preserve the connections discovered while learning them.

The project may eventually be useful to other curious learners, but general-purpose scale must not weaken its personal and exploratory character.

## What success looks like

Hanzi Explorer succeeds when:

- Returning to the atlas makes previously learned characters feel more connected and memorable.
- Adding a character creates a durable knowledge artifact rather than another isolated note.
- Components and character families reveal relationships that ordinary vocabulary lists obscure.
- Historical, literary, and personal context encourages further exploration.
- The atlas remains pleasurable and simple enough to revisit regularly.

Character count alone is not a success metric. Depth, connection, usefulness, and continued curiosity matter more.

## Product principles

### Visual first

The character is the central object. The interface should be calm, beautiful, and spacious enough to invite close attention.

### Understanding over drilling

Hanzi Explorer complements tools such as HelloChinese, Pleco, and Anki. It does not need to reproduce their lessons, dictionaries, or spaced-repetition systems.

### Personal before comprehensive

The atlas records Simon’s learning journey. It does not need to contain every Chinese character or serve every kind of learner.

### Relationships over isolated facts

Components, related characters, phrases, stories, and literary appearances should gradually turn the collection into a connected knowledge system.

### Grow without unnecessary machinery

Prefer static, inspectable data and straightforward technology while those choices support the experience. Add infrastructure only when a real product need requires it.

### Preserve serendipity

The project began through an unexpected path involving Dunhuang and 典故. The product should leave room for curiosity, side paths, and surprising connections.

## Strategic tracks

### The specimen atlas

Continue improving the core character records and the experience of browsing and studying individual specimens.

### Character relationships

Reveal families, shared components, semantic relationships, and paths between characters already in the atlas.

### Tang Lens

Connect characters to Tang poetry and allow literary texts to become entry points into the atlas.

### Personal knowledge

Develop better ways to preserve mnemonics, observations, discoveries, and connections made during learning.

## Not currently pursuing

- A comprehensive Chinese dictionary
- A replacement for HelloChinese or Pleco
- A general-purpose flashcard or spaced-repetition system
- Social features or a learner community
- Premature accounts, syncing, or backend infrastructure
- Growth in character count at the expense of specimen quality
